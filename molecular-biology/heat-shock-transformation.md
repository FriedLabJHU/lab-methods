# Transformation by heat shock

1. Remove the aliquot of competent cells from -80 °C and thaw on ice (takes 10-20 minutes).
2. While thawing, prepare microfuge tubes for transformations. Take out one microfuge tube per transfer and place it into the tube block. Transfer 0.5-1 µL of plasmid DNA (~10 ng) or PCR product (~100 ng) to the microfuge tube.
3. Immerse the water bath tube rack into the ice bucket.
4. Add 25-50 µL of competent cells to the microfuge tube, gently aspirating 4-5 times to mix. Try to minimize the time cells are off-ice.
5. Incubate cells on ice for 20-30 min.
6. Heat shock at 42 °C / 40 s, and then put back on ice for 2 min.
7. Add 0.5-1 mL SOC medium to each tube.
8. Recover by incubating at 37 °C / 700 rpm in a thermomixer. (Optimal: 40-90 min)
9. While recovering is a good time to prepare your agar plates if you haven't already

   a. Microwave LB Agar for 10 min

   b. Cool down molten LB Agar by incubating at 42°C for a few min.

   c. Add drugs and mix well by inverting or swishing in the bottle (make sure you do not add drugs at high temperature to avoid it getting degraded)

   d. Dispense 20-25 mL per plate with a serological pipette

10. Plate some fraction of the recovery onto LB Agar dishes (typically 20-200 uL, which can vary depending on how many colonies you'd expect to get).
11. Spread onto plates using coli-rollers (or streak out if you're afraid the colonies would be too dense)
12. Incubate at 37 °C overnight

    > **Note:** If you are working with toxic DNA or are having trouble with the transformation, you can add 0.5% glucose in LB agar (glucose can repress the Lac operator) and incubate the LB agar plates at 30 ºC instead of 37 ˚C.  It typically takes >=24 h for colonies to appear at 30˚C.
