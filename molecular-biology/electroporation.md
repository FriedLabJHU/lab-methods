# Transformation by electroporation

1. Remove the aliquot of competent cells from ~80°C and thaw on ice (takes 10-20 min).
2. Label and pre-cool 2 mm electrocuvettes with a blue cap on ice (one per transformation).
3. Label microfuge tubes (one per transformation) and place them into tube block. Transfer 0.5-1 µL of plasmid DNA (~10 ng) to a microfuge tube.
4. Add 25-50 uL of competent cells to the microfuge tube, gently aspirating 4-5 times to mix, and then transfer the DNA/cell mixture into the correspondingly labeled electrocuvette. Keep electrocuvettes on ice.
5. Put the electrocuvette into an electroporation chamber and immediately electroporate at 2500 V (for linear DNA with very high transformation efficiency, you can use 1 mm electrocuvette and electroporate at 2000 V). Time constants ≥ 5.0 ms are ideal.
6. Immediately after electroporating, add 0.5-1 mL SOC medium to the microfuge tube with the corresponding label.
7. Recover by incubating at 37°C / 700 rpm (Optimal: 40-90 min).
8. Whilst recovering is a good time to prepare your agar plates if you haven't already

   a. Microwave LB Agar for 10 min.

   b. Cool down molten LB Agar by incubating at 42°C for a few min.

   c. Add drugs and mix well by inverting or swishing in the bottle.

   d. Dispense 20-25 mL of LB agar by a serological pipette.

9. Plate some fraction of the recovery to the LB Agar dishes (typically 20-200 µL, which can vary depending on how many colonies you'd expect to get).
10. Spread onto plates using coil-rollers (or streak out if you're afraid the colonies would be too dense).
11. Incubate at 37 °C overnight.

    > **Note:** If you are working with toxic DNA or are having trouble with the transformation, you can add 0.5% glucose in LB agar (glucose can repress the Lac operator) and incubate the LB agar plates at 30 ºC instead of 37 ˚C.  It typically takes >=24 h for colonies to appear at 30˚C.
